# Restoring a 1970 Puch Maxi

Category: Build and Research

- What I restored or changed:I broke most things on the bike before fixing anything. It had to get much much worse before it got better. This allowed me to understand the basics of combustions engines and vehicles. Blunders from big to little, like shearing bolts in the engine block, bending the frame with excess heat, but ultimately all was fixed.
- Where and when: bought in cambridge and eventually driven all the way down to London, including 2 hours of pushing when frame bent and chain came off.
- My role and starting skill level: knew nothing when I started, learnt electrics, mechanics, engine, fuel lines, etc. etc...
- Why it matters to me: a vehicle with buckets of character, might not work every time you pick it up, took years to get it working and still not sure about it, which if anything is a metaphor for life and our projects. You can give up anytime you like. Which I nearly did. Or you can hold onto the hope that if you keep going, you will get to scoot around town looking epic eventually.
Also there's an element of fighting the beast. You rarely have the right tool, angle, or leverage. You need to bolt the thing on, or off, spring load it, etc... there is not correct way so that the process is easy. You will hurt your hands doing it. You might still fail. You will try every angle and method and bodge, and all your effort might only just allow you to connect things up the right way. In a sense, it is like weight lifting: you can't sidestep the challenge.
- Strongest technical challenge or breakthrough: having sheared bolts drilled out of engine block and gaps rethreaded.
