# Project page intake system

This folder is the staging area for unfinished projects on the Projects page.

## The quick workflow

1. Open the folder for the project you want to build.
2. Add a few rough answers to its `PROJECT.md`. Ordinary notes are fine; polished copy is not required.
3. Drop source material into the appropriate folder under `files`:
   - `images`
   - `documents`
   - `audio`
   - `video`
   - `misc`
4. Tell Codex: **Build the [project name] page from its intake folder.**

The raw `files` folders are deliberately excluded from GitHub and deployment. Codex will select from them, make lightweight publishable derivatives, remove private metadata, and keep the originals local.

If a project contains several distinct chapters, use a folder for each chapter inside `files`. Horses is the model: polo and farming have their own spaces, while every named trail has a separate folder under `files/trails`. Music is separate from Choir and may similarly grow through chapters such as guitar.

## Minimum useful context

Only six facts are genuinely useful:

1. What happened or what did you make?
2. Where and when?
3. What was your role?
4. Why does it matter to you?
5. What is the strongest moment, result, or anecdote?
6. Is anything private, misleading, or not for publication?

Anything else can usually be inferred from the materials and filenames.

The best protection against generic or over-written copy is one or two phrases in your own language. If a sentence, diary line, caption, joke, technical expression, or remembered remark captures the project, put it in `PROJECT.md` exactly as you would say it. Also list any words or tones you dislike.

## Filename signals

Do not rename everything. Add a short signal only when it helps:

- `thumbnail` — strongest project-card image
- `good` — visually strong and worth prioritising
- `official` — formal or documentary
- `process` — making, rehearsing, repairing, preparing
- `before` / `after` — transformation
- `detail` — a useful close-up
- `fun` — informal; use sparingly
- `context` — place, people, programme, map, or surrounding event
- `private` — never publish

Examples:

- `thumbnail-finished-motorbike.jpg`
- `good-crossing-the-carpathians.jpeg`
- `process-cleaning-the-carburettor.mov`
- `official-camp-group-photo.jpg`
- `fun-kitchen-after-service.jpg`
- `private-address-list.pdf`

Order matters only for audio tracks or an intentional sequence. In those cases, begin filenames with `01-`, `02-`, and so on.

## Documents and text

Upload original PDFs, programmes, journal entries, scans, presentations, certificates, routes, maps, or rough notes. A `notes.txt` file can contain unstructured memories, captions, names, dates, corrections, and half-written thoughts.

Codex should use documents as source material, not automatically embed every document in the finished page.

## How pages will differ

The intake format is consistent; the resulting pages should not be.

- A journey may become a map, diary, route, or photographic sequence.
- A restoration may use before/after comparisons and a build timeline.
- Painting may use a quiet gallery with detail views and process studies.
- Dancing may centre video, performance context, and a small photo essay.
- Horses may combine encounters, work, skill and place.
- Cycling or pilgrimage may use distance, route, stages and reflection.
- Service projects should foreground people, purpose, responsibilities and outcomes without becoming self-congratulatory.

## Review rule

The first build should be a strong editorial draft. Afterwards, review:

- factual names, dates and credits;
- anything too generic or over-written;
- media that should be swapped or removed;
- whether the page sounds like you.

Generic headings should be treated as placeholders during the first build. When the source material contains a memorable phrase or specific detail, prefer that over headings such as “The journey”, “The experience”, or “A shared purpose”.
